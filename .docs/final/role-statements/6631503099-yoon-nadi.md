I owned the user-facing design of YummyNaKha!: how a diner moves from a food
profile to a Thai menu photo, to three result groups, to ordering in Thai.

My main design rule was safety wording: no screen says a dish is "safe", no
score is shown, and warnings point to restaurant staff.

## Reflection

**1. Lessons learned.** Building YummyNaKha! with AI changed how I work as a
designer. **What got better: speed.** With Figma Make I built a clickable
prototype of 12 screens in a few days instead of weeks, so we could show the
whole flow and test it early. Claude Code also helped our team keep the spec,
backlog and design in sync. **What got worse: AI makes work look finished
before it is.** My prototype looked complete, but it was missing a consent
screen and account deletion. I had to list gaps G1–G9 by hand. Our first
version used Gemini for ingredients, and the same photo gave the same
ingredients for only 6 of 16 dishes. We also had numbers on our slides with no
file behind them, and we had to remove them. Testing with real people was still
needed: 9 of 16 testers misread "Check First", and 8 of them thought it meant
"safe". AI did not catch that. People did.

**2. Where I stand today.** I am a junior designer at the start of my career.
AI can now make screens quickly, so making screens is no longer enough. My
value is understanding users, checking what AI produces, and choosing careful
words, like never calling a dish "safe". I can design, prototype with AI, read
our code and use test results. I still need more practice in user research and
accessibility.

**3. My plan.** Next year I want a **UX/UI internship**, with YummyNaKha! as
the main case study in my portfolio. I want to learn how real teams run user
research and usability tests. In five years I want to be a **senior product
designer** who leads the design of a product. I will use AI tools every day, but
I will make sure that every design is tested with real users and is honest about
what the product can and cannot do.
