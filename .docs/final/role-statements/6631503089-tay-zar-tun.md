## Reflection

**1. Lessons learned.** For me, building with AI made almost everything better. Our
requirements, backlog and design documents were done in about two hours,
coding was much faster, and AI wrote test scripts that gave us real evidence, not a
test plan on paper. The thing that got worse was hallucination. AI can produce work
that looks finished but is wrong: our first version let Gemini guess ingredients,
and it gave the same answer for only 6 of 16 dishes across three scans. We had to
check everything, and we handled a lot of it. My biggest lesson as Product Owner is
that we must make the decisions ourselves. When we didn't decide clearly, AI started
building its own idea, not what we wanted. Decisions like "fixed rules decide
allergies, never AI" had to come from us.

**2. Where I stand.** I am a junior software engineer, but with AI I feel I can do
more than a normal junior could do a year ago. Instead of spending most of my time
hard-coding, I can review work, understand the whole system, and make decisions on
the project together with my team. I think this is where a junior's value is moving
in the AI era: judgement and responsibility, not only typing code.

**3. My plan.** Next year, after my co-op internship ends in April, I want a
full-time job as a full-stack developer or software engineer, in a good company or
my dream company, where I can get a lot of real working experience. In five years,
after growing into a senior software engineer or tech lead role, I imagine myself
as the founder of my own IT startup, building AI-related products, which is
something I have always wanted to do.
